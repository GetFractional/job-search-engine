# 10 Authority and Artifact Disposition

## Authority decision

The strategy cannot govern design while older hubs, screen counts, names, and prototype claims remain silently coequal. This manifest reconciles the current library without deleting user work.

Disposition states:

- **Controls:** current authority for the stated decision.
- **Incorporated:** useful content was reconciled into a controlling chapter; the older file is evidence, not authority.
- **Reference only:** may inform implementation or history, but cannot overrule the strategy.
- **Superseded:** intentionally replaced; do not use for a new decision or user handoff.
- **Unresolved conflict:** requires a named decision before implementation.

## Unresolved before production implementation

- My Way Ahead comprehension, recall, domain control, collision review, and formal legal clearance,
- observed differentiation from RoleWorth, Waypoint, Worklore, Umon, CareerLedger, and free alternatives,
- exact buy-versus-build choices for commodity infrastructure and the sequence of proprietary product slices,
- permitted source acquisition methods and licensing,
- production identity, storage, encryption, deletion, incident, and vendor architecture,
- field-specific model error budgets and slice evidence,
- approved pricing, refund, renewal, and billing terms,
- moderated comprehension, accessibility, and mobile usability results,
- whether the complete subscription earns recurring billing across active-search and monitoring periods.

These unresolved items do not block research, concierge delivery, synthetic prototyping, or Matt's controlled founder dogfooding through the same member product. They do block a public product claim, efficacy claim, or production commitment that depends on them.

## Supersession and cleanup rule

When a controlling decision changes:

1. update the canonical chapter,
2. record the evidence and decision in the Strategy Center,
3. rebuild both PDFs,
4. mark the older artifact or decision superseded,
5. update dependent briefs and tests,
6. preserve history until a separate cleanup review confirms deletion is safe; never delete solely because an artifact is superseded, and protect unrelated user work and useful evidence.
