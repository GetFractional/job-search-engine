# 01 Market, Customer, and Competition Intelligence

## Decision

The market does not need more job volume. It needs a better way to identify fresh, legitimate, high-upside opportunities, compare them with the person's real baseline, and turn the few good decisions into safe, effective action.

The category is crowded and converging quickly. Free and paid products already offer job aggregation, alerts, resume import, match explanations, ATS scoring, autofill, tracking, document generation, interview support, review-before-submit, and increasingly agentic search. Feature overlap does not remove the customer need. The initial company wins only if people can complete the important job more accurately, clearly, quickly, and confidently, then keep receiving useful value across a career.

## Market pressures that create the opportunity

| Pressure | Evidence | Product consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Competition for roles has intensified | LinkedIn reports U.S. applicants per open role doubled from spring 2022 to 2026, while 65% of respondents said finding a job had become harder. | Help users allocate effort and understand pursuit feasibility; do not reward application volume. |
| Many professionals are looking or quietly watching | Gallup reports 51% of U.S. employees were actively looking or watching for a new job, while only 28% believed it was a good time to find a quality role. | A passive-monitoring use case is behaviorally credible, but willingness to pay still requires proof. |
| People doubt whether jobs are real | LinkedIn's 2026 safety research says 72% question job legitimacy at least sometimes, and 47% check the employer's official careers site. FTC data show rapidly rising losses from job and employment scams. | Canonical employer, exact requisition, source state, freshness, duplicate, wrapper, and scam signals belong in every decision. |
| Fit uncertainty is costly | LinkedIn identifies uncertainty about skills and fit as a major difficulty; person-job fit research also distinguishes the job, organization, group, and supervisor rather than treating fit as keyword similarity. | Evaluate mandate, environment, trajectory, constraints, evidence, and economics, not only resume overlap. |
| Skills and career paths are changing | The World Economic Forum expects substantial job disruption and skill change through 2030. Google now provides free AI-assisted transferable-skill and career-path exploration. | Role discovery must model adjacency, transferability, learnable gaps, and future optionality. |
| Search feedback is weak | Greenhouse and Gallup report widespread ghosting and low interview yield. | Treat silence as missing signal, not proof that the candidate or strategy failed. Capture stage-specific outcomes and objections. |
| Job search is mobile | Indeed reports the majority of its applications occur on mobile and continues to build mobile search, alerts, tracking, messaging, and preparation. | Mobile must make discovery, triage, corrections, recruiter response, and progress effortless. Dense evidence and document work needs a strong desktop mode. |
| AI trust is fragile | Teal's own terms warn that AI output may be inaccurate and requires user verification; customer reviews across career tools cite robotic writing, forced keywords, autofill omissions, irrelevant jobs, and billing friction. | Trust must be demonstrated through product behavior, state language, correction, approval, and transparent billing. |

## What customers have already tried

| Alternative | Why they try it | Where it breaks | What the product must do better |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn, Indeed, and job boards | Broad supply, recruiter access, alerts, simple applications | Noise, wrappers, duplicated or stale roles, social comparison, weak career-baseline context | Source quality, freshness, restraint, economic comparison, and network-aware next actions |
| Teal, Huntr, Careerflow, JobFlow | Organization, tracking, tailoring, reminders, integrated workflow | The job decision remains spread across match scores, user judgment, and manual research | Make decision policy, uncertainty, source integrity, and one next action coherent |
| RoleWorth | Decide-before-tailoring score, source and liveness checks, Radar, proof-backed packets, tracking, and approval-gated submission | It already occupies the most obvious decision-first product position and packages much of the proposed visible workflow at free-to-low monthly prices | Do not claim decision-first scoring, source checks, proof, approval, Radar, or career memory as unique; validate a senior, baseline-relative, expert-guided wedge |
| Jobscan, Rezi, resume builders | ATS reassurance, keywords, fast documents | Keyword forcing, generic writing, local optimization of an incomplete JD, weak causal proof | Treat documents as a consequence of a good decision and approved evidence |
| Simplify and browser autofill | Reduce repetitive form work | Missing or incorrect fields, inventory gaps, opaque automation, review burden | Use typed field data, exact payload review, correction, and user-controlled automation |
| Jobright, Sonara, auto-apply tools | Save time and increase volume | Irrelevant submissions, unclear limits, listing quality, reputation risk, billing distrust | Optimize for qualified pursuit and explicit control, not raw application count |
| Spreadsheets, notes, browser tabs | Flexibility and ownership | Fragmentation, duplicate work, no source freshness, no learning loop | Create portable structured career memory without hiding the underlying facts |
| ChatGPT or general AI | Cheap research, drafting, brainstorming | Context drift, unsupported claims, inconsistent extraction, weak state management | Versioned sources, stable policy, evaluation, provenance, and persistent approved truth |
| Career coaching | Judgment, accountability, confidence, negotiation | Expensive, inconsistent, episodic, hard to evaluate | Productize repeatable judgment and use experts at high-value inflection points |
| Recruiters and marketplaces | Access, introductions, perceived curation | Employer incentives, uneven fit, limited market coverage, little user-owned memory | Remain user-aligned, portable, and explicit about incentives and evidence |

Customer-review evidence is directional, not statistically representative. Samples, invited-review programs, and current product states vary. The recurring pattern across Teal, Huntr, Simplify, Jobscan, Jobright, and Sonara is that users value saved time and organization, but distrust weak output, irrelevant roles, automation errors, and surprise billing.

### Current price ladder

| Market band | Typical offer | Observed signal |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Discovery, tracking, alerts, basic documents, exploration | LinkedIn, Indeed, EarnBetter, Google Career Dreamer, many suite free tiers |
| About $13 to $50 per month | Active-search workflow, AI documents, ATS analysis, autofill, tracking, and decision-first job scoring | Teal, Huntr, Simplify+, Careerflow, Jobscan, Rezi, RoleWorth Sprint and Pro |
| About $149 lifetime | Narrow document utility | Rezi |
| About $199 to $1,099 one time | Human-completed applications or guided search labor | Scale.jobs and concierge services |
| About $199 to $739 one time | Human-built senior career intelligence and target-market maps | Waypoint Discovery and Starter |
| About $1,250 to $5,000 | Negotiation and high-stakes expert help | Levels.fyi services |

The ladder suggests two things. First, commodity AI, tracking, and now decision-first job scoring create a low monthly ceiling. Second, high-stakes judgment and labor substitution can command far more. The business should validate software value and expert value separately.

## Closest-competitor reality check

RoleWorth is the current closest visible product competitor, not a distant analogy. Its public product already claims or demonstrates:

- job search and capture,
- source, liveness, salary clarity, and ghost-risk checks,
- a job-worth score and Pursue / Maybe / Skip routing,
- proof-backed resume and application packages,
- explicit approval before supported ATS submission,
- tracking, reminders, company briefs, and outcome memory,
- a Job Market Radar, Chrome side panel, and installable mobile web app,
- free, weekly, monthly, and lifetime price options.

This evidence removes several former differentiation claims. `Decision first`, `the job earns the effort`, `proof before polishing`, `human approval`, `Radar`, and `career memory` may still be sound product principles, but they are no longer a market position by themselves.

The launch thesis therefore becomes narrower:

> **Help an experienced professional decide whether one consequential move is materially better than their current career baseline, then build the strongest honest pursuit with expert judgment.**

The competitive squeeze is broader than RoleWorth:

- Worklore already positions a persistent career-intelligence layer across opportunities, relationships, and a career library.
- Umon already uses Career Vault, Skill Graph, evidence, trajectory, matching, and grounded preparation.
- CareerLedger targets professionals with 10-plus years of experience using multi-version career narrative, AI positioning, expert guidance, and matching.
- Waypoint sells human-built career intelligence to senior professionals, including market maps, target companies, decision-makers, hiring signals, and outreach strategy.

Public evidence supports a more useful benchmark than a feature checklist. `Demonstrated` here means a detailed first-party workflow, public specimen, or official app-store surface; it does not mean output accuracy was independently validated inside a paid account.

| Job the product must perform | Current public benchmark | Quality bar this company must exceed |
|---|---|---|
| Separate requirements from preferences | Huntr documents must-have versus nice-to-have weighting and user ignore controls | Preserve modality, OR/AND logic, equivalent experience, conditional requirements, and source spans without silent inversion |
| Structure ambiguous job text | Rezi separates responsibilities, requirements, skills, salary, benefits, company, and EEO content | Keep compensation equity distinct from pay-equity or DEI language; represent missing and conflicting fields explicitly |
| Explain source and freshness | Role Call shows verified-remote, repost, salary, company-stage, pursue reasons, and watch-outs | Resolve the canonical requisition, retain source history and diffs, show field lineage, and make stale or conflicting evidence recoverable |
| Complete frequent tasks on mobile | Jobright ships native mobile discovery, match, alert, analysis, and tracking | Make capture, triage, correction, decision, and approval one-handed and complete without hiding dense evidence |
| Preserve truth and ownership | Career Ledger documents verified facts, evidence, undo, revocable links, export, and read-only retention after cancellation | Propagate corrections, invalidate stale outputs and approvals, preserve audit history, and keep user data portable |
| Explain a score honestly | Jobscan publishes score components and states its Match Rate is not an employer ATS score | Separate Integrity, Move Value, and Pursuit Readiness; show sensitivity, confidence limits, and what would change the recommendation |
| Monitor a senior market | Waypoint delivers human-curated company, contact, and hiring-signal refreshes | Surface only verified material changes or opportunities that beat the user's baseline, including credible no-action value |

The full-funnel prototype should test a more complete job: deciding whether one known move is materially better than the person's current baseline, then constructing the strongest truthful pursuit. It should still provide any discovery, tracking, proof, preparation, or monitoring capability the target persona materially needs. The rule is not `avoid what competitors do`; it is `earn trust by doing the whole job unusually well and making quality observable`.

The company-level build decision is settled: own the customer product, brand, career model, decision policy, evidence system, corrections, and learning loop. Founder-operated cases and expert services accelerate paid learning while the owned product is built. Commodity infrastructure may be integrated behind portable interfaces, but licensing or white-labeling another career platform is not an alternative company strategy.

## Required table stakes

Do not use these as primary differentiation, but do not omit them when they are necessary to complete the user's job. Each must earn its place through task-level quality benchmarks:

- resume or profile import,
- job preferences and saved searches,
- alerts and recurring scans,
- generic match scores,
- missing-keyword explanations,
- job tracking and reminders,
- AI resumes, bullets, cover letters, and application answers,
- browser autofill,
- review-before-submit,
- generic interview practice,
- contact or referral suggestions,
- salary data,
- broad `career copilot`, `career OS`, `career intelligence`, or `all-in-one` language.

These requirements are evaluated task by task. A competitor mentioning a feature does not establish that it works well, and a better product cannot remove a necessary task merely to appear different. The evidence standard is successful completion on shared realistic and adversarial cases, including correction and recovery.

## Remaining differentiation territory to validate

None of this is presumed defensible. It is the territory where customer and paid-delivery evidence could establish a meaningful difference.

### 1. Baseline-relative career economics

Compare the possible move with the user's current role and credible alternatives across base pay, variable compensation, equity uncertainty, family benefits, flexibility, commute and travel, stability, mandate, career capital, and downside. A job can be legitimate and attainable without being a better move.

### 2. Senior-market specialization and human judgment

Start with experienced growth, revenue, GTM operations, lifecycle, and ecommerce leaders. Build the self-serve product around an expert-calibrated method, then offer a separately priced proof interview or human review when ambiguity is consequential. Measure every intervention so expert support never hides weak software.

### 3. Fresh, canonical opportunity intelligence

Resolve the real employer and exact requisition. Deduplicate wrappers. Separate official publication date, first seen, repost, and last verified. Suppress inactive or unverified roles. Show source health and scam risk.

### 4. Decision quality beyond resume similarity

Judge whether the move is worth wanting and worth the effort. Compare compensation, benefits, mandate, trajectory, work environment, logistics, company risk, network access, learning, optionality, freshness, and pursuit cost with the user's current baseline.

### 5. Honest negative evidence

Tell the user why a role may not be worth pursuing, which conclusion is inferred, what is unknown, and what could invalidate the recommendation. An honest no-action week must still feel valuable.

### 6. Transferability without invention

Separate:

- proven now,
- credibly transferable,
- attainable through a bounded gap,
- unsupported,
- deliberately excluded by the user.

A pure proof gate can trap career changers and stretch candidates in their current lane. Potential should be explicit and bounded, never disguised as proof.

### 7. Closed-loop career learning

Learn from user corrections, decisions, screens, interviews, objections, offers, compensation, and job changes. Do not infer too much from silence. Preserve the original decision and evidence versions so learning never rewrites history.

### 8. Portable career memory

Maintain verified achievements, stories, role preferences, compensation history, proof gaps, market reactions, and approved language. Users must be able to inspect, correct, export, and delete it.

### 9. Human escalation at consequential moments

Use qualified experts for complex career reframing, final materials, interviews, offers, and negotiation. Human intervention should be an explicit service layer, not a hidden substitute for unreliable automation.

## Strategic category and competitive position

### Internal category

**Career Opportunity Decision System**

### Plain-language category

**A career opportunity platform for deciding, finding, and pursuing better moves, with expert help when it matters.**

### Positioning statement

For experienced growth and revenue leaders considering a consequential move, the platform compares real opportunities with the person's current career and economic baseline, explains each decision with honest evidence and uncertainty, and helps build the strongest defensible pursuit. Unlike broad job-search suites and shallow match scores, the launch experience is specialized, baseline-relative, easy to audit, and supported by expert judgment when ambiguity is consequential.

### Strategic contrast

| The category often optimizes | This platform should optimize |
|---|---|
| More matches | Fewer opportunities worth attention |
| Resume similarity | Move value, readiness, and integrity |
| Application volume | Qualified pursuit allocation |
| Fast generation | Truthful, decision-specific preparation |
| One search episode | Compounding career memory |
| Opaque automation | Explicit, revocable user control |
| Employer or marketplace liquidity | User-aligned independent judgment |

## Market risks and counterarguments

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation or test |
|---|---|---|
| A near-direct competitor already ships most visible features | RoleWorth publicly combines decision-first scoring, source and liveness checks, proof-backed packets, approval, Radar, tracking, and memory at low prices | Run head-to-head tasks; let one complete free self-serve decision prove the difference, then test $49 monthly and $119 for three months plus separate expert offers; stop if customers see no material difference or will not pay |
| Adjacent products occupy every broad story | Worklore and Umon claim career memory and evidence; CareerLedger targets experienced professionals; Waypoint sells senior human-led career intelligence | Avoid broad category claims; test one sharply bounded job and compare the full offer, not a feature checklist |
| Large platforms copy the visible features | LinkedIn, Teal, Jobscan, Jobright, and others already converge rapidly | Build proprietary learning from corrections and outcomes; stay source-agnostic; prove decision value, not feature novelty |
| Direct-source coverage is hard or legally constrained | Job sources change, block access, and impose terms | Start with user-supplied and direct-employer sources plus visible operator support; use permitted feeds and links; maintain source receipts; seek counsel before scaling acquisition |
| Users may prefer free tools | Basic search and drafting are abundant | Make the free experience prove integrity and one complete self-serve decision; charge for repeated decisions, Radar, Pursuit, career memory, and separately disclosed expert help |
| Passive monitoring may not retain paid subscribers | People watch the market, but long-term paid behavior is not proven | Include achievement memory and market movement; compare monthly renewal with three-month term use and renewal; make no annual commitment until value beyond active search is proven |
| Selectivity can reduce visible activity | Users may equate fewer results with less value | Show reviewed, suppressed, unresolved, and why; make no-action value concrete |
| Proof can overfit the past | Users want upward moves and career changes | Model transferability, bounded gaps, market demand, and potential separately from proven experience |
| AI errors can destroy trust | Career and compensation decisions are consequential | Golden-data thresholds, critical-field human review, correction invalidation, degraded states, incident response |
| A service-backed start may hide weak software | Concierge work can compensate for confusing UX or unreliable automation | Separate operator intervention from product success, instrument every intervention, and require self-serve task benchmarks before scale |

## Evidence gaps to close

- Direct interviews with at least 15 target professionals across active and passive modes.
- Paid demand from at least five users, not only stated interest.
- Observed value of a no-opportunity Radar result.
- Direct-source coverage, duplicate rate, and freshness failure rate for the initial role lanes.
- Comparison test between a generic match card and the complete Decision Receipt.
- Head-to-head test against RoleWorth or another decision-first product on the same roles, with task comprehension, trust, decision change, setup burden, and willingness to pay measured.
- Buy-versus-build boundaries for commodity infrastructure after ten paid cases, including data portability, privacy, support burden, vendor switching, and time to market; the owned-product decision itself is not reopened.
- Longitudinal evidence that career memory improves a later decision or reduces setup.
- Current competitive refresh immediately before public pricing or investor claims.
