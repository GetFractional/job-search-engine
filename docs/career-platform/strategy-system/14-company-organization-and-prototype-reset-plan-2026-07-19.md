# 14 Company Organization and Prototype Experience Reset

**Decision date:** 2026-07-19
**Board:** Matt Dimock
**Accountable CEO and integrator:** Codex root, within the existing authority contract
**Mission:** Prototype Comprehension and Experience Reset
**Status:** Approved local research and planning. Prices, external testing, hosting, paid services, production data, and public release remain Board gates.

## Executive decision

Do not polish the current prototype screen by screen. Freeze it as the baseline and rebuild the structural experience around two ordinary customer jobs:

1. I have a job in mind. Help me decide whether it is worth pursuing.
2. I want something better. Help me define it, find it, and act when the right opportunity appears.

Retain the selected Executive Evidence visual identity, warm light theme, dark theme, restrained mint action color, editorial typography, and connected Integrity Preview timeline. Change the density, hierarchy, copy, route order, interaction model, personalization, commercial architecture, and quality gates.

The active company organization is no longer a list of generic expert lenses. It is a matrix organization with permanent departments and actual accountability roles, plus temporary cross-functional mission teams. The titles below are virtual operating seats today. They are not employees, licensed professionals, statutory officers, or authority to bind Matt or a future company.

## Customer and persona model

The initial paid beachhead remains experienced U.S. professionals with meaningful economic upside and difficult tradeoffs. The copy and data model must not tell other people that the product is not for them.

| Persona mode | Trigger | Most important outcome | Product emphasis | Key data differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Established active seeker | Needs a stronger role soon | Find and pursue the best credible job without wasting time | Fresh jobs, baseline comparison, readiness, pursuit | Base, bonus, equity, benefits, mandate, level, location, family constraints |
| Established passive watcher | Employed but open | Be alerted only when a role is materially better | Quiet monitoring, no-action value, market movement | Upside threshold, notification restraint, privacy, current-role baseline |
| Career changer | Current path is no longer acceptable | See credible adjacent directions and the exact gaps | Transferability, lane comparison, skill and proof plan | Alternative requirements, learning cost, level reset, bridge roles |
| Level-up professional | Wants the next level | Understand readiness and build proof before applying | Scope, leadership evidence, compensation, timing | Mandate depth, team/budget scope, influence, missing proof |
| Early-career user | Needs direction and momentum | Avoid dead ends and choose reachable opportunities | Simpler onboarding, learning value, realistic requirements | Internships, projects, education, location, first-job constraints |
| Hourly or shift worker | Needs better weekly economics and life fit | Compare real take-home, schedule, commute, stability, and benefits | Fast capture, weekly earnings, availability, immediate next step | Hourly rate, tips, shifts, hours, commute, childcare, eligibility waiting periods |

The modes change emphasis and fields, not the dignity or quality of the experience.

## Corrected end-to-end customer journeys

### Journey A: I have a job to check

1. Home explains the outcome and asks for a link or job description.
2. The system immediately checks source, employer, freshness, duplicate risk, visible pay, and unresolved facts.
3. The user sees a useful Integrity Preview before creating an account.
4. Account creation saves that result.
5. The product asks only for profile facts and preferences that could change this decision.
6. The user confirms an editable Career Baseline.
7. The product delivers one complete job decision free, including reason, risk, unknowns, and next step.
8. The user chooses Pursue, Watch, or Pass.
9. Continued software or optional human help is offered only after this value is delivered.
10. A Pursue decision begins preparation. It never means application submission.

### Journey B: Help me find something better

1. Home asks what would help most now.
2. The user creates an account and imports or enters minimum experience.
3. The user defines what "better" means across pay, work, growth, risk, and constraints.
4. The product suggests several credible directions with reasons and gaps.
5. The user keeps one focus and optional secondary directions.
6. The product creates a plain-language career plan.
7. When source and value gates are met, the user configures Opportunity Radar.
8. Radar produces restrained briefs, including a useful "nothing better right now" result.
9. Each surfaced job enters the same decision and pursuit path as Journey A.

## Screen contract for the reset

Every screen must specify:

- the one user job,
- the one primary decision or action,
- information required before that action,
- details hidden behind progressive disclosure,
- source of truth,
- empty, loading, error, partial, stale, conflict, success, and offline states,
- mobile and desktop behavior,
- keyboard, focus, zoom, contrast, motion, and assistive-technology behavior,
- analytics event and success measure,
- acceptance test and responsible owner.

## Evaluation plan

### Phase 0: Freeze and measure the baseline - complete

**Owners:** Product, UX, Engineering, Independent Quality
**Outputs:** Current route inventory, screenshot set, defect list, source findings, test results
**Result:** The current build is preserved as the comparison baseline. Nine P1 blockers are documented in the 2026-07-19 audit.

### Phase 1: Correct the journey and language

**Owners:** Head of Product, Service Design, Content Design, Product Marketing, Career Intelligence
**Work:**

1. Map both core journeys and every branch, back path, edit path, save point, interruption, and commercial transition.
2. Define one user job and one primary action per screen.
3. Move technical trust detail behind ordinary-language summaries.
4. Replace internal terms with a controlled plain-language vocabulary.
5. Rebuild homepage information architecture around outcome, mechanism, example, trust, personas, offer preview, objections, and action.
6. Separate public website navigation from authenticated product navigation.

**Gate:** At least 80 percent of target participants can explain what the product does, who it helps, why it is different, and what to do first after a brief homepage exposure.

### Phase 2: Rebuild the mobile interaction system

**Owners:** Principal Mobile Product Designer, Design Systems and Accessibility Lead, Product Engineer
**Work:**

1. Fix the job-entry component and all URL, pasted-description, error, success, long-content, and keyboard states.
2. Apply the approved mobile spacing and typography system.
3. Create an onboarding progress shell with Back, Edit, autosave, and resume behavior.
4. Reduce repeated card chrome and make decision hierarchy visually dominant.
5. Put deep evidence behind expandable sections without hiding material risk.
6. Preserve the selected visual identity in Light, Dark, and System modes.
7. Remove internal fixture and model controls from user Settings.

**Gate:** No clipping, horizontal overflow, trapped scroll, keyboard-hidden primary action, or touch target below 44 pixels at 320, 390, 768, and desktop widths. Core tasks must work at 200 percent zoom and by keyboard.

### Phase 3: Rebuild the first-value and decision spine

**Owners:** Head of Product, Career Decision Scientist, Product Engineer, Applied AI and Evaluation Lead
**Build slices, in order:**

1. Home and job capture.
2. Integrity Preview and save/account transition.
3. Minimum experience and editable Career Baseline.
4. Role directions and plain-language career plan.
5. Opportunity summary and progressive deep review.
6. Pursue, Watch, and Pass behavior.
7. Materials and exact external-approval boundary.
8. Radar setup and brief only after the monitoring value gate remains credible.

**Gate:** At least 85 percent complete onboarding without help. At least 85 percent correctly explain the recommendation, strongest reason, biggest risk, uncertainty, and next action.

### Phase 4: Prove job interpretation and scoring quality

**Owners:** Applied AI and Evaluation Lead, Career Data Ontology Architect, Recruiter, Compensation Analyst, Independent AI and Career QA
**Gold fixture set:**

- direct employer posting versus aggregator wrapper,
- stale or removed posting,
- base plus bonus,
- stock or ownership equity versus diversity or fairness language,
- benefits offered versus eligibility or marketing language,
- required versus preferred experience,
- degree or equivalent-experience alternatives,
- remote, hybrid, travel, and location ambiguity,
- hourly rate, tips, shift, and weekly earnings,
- compensation missing or conflicting across sources,
- application-only questions,
- several real job-description structures and lengths.

**Minimum benchmark hypotheses:**

- requirement extraction F1 of at least 0.90,
- compensation-component precision of at least 0.98,
- benefit-state precision of at least 0.95,
- zero known cases where EEO "equity" becomes compensation,
- every unknown remains unknown until evidence resolves it,
- unchanged evidence produces stable scores,
- each material recommendation traces to job evidence and the approved user baseline.

These thresholds are internal release hypotheses. Career and model reviewers may raise them when the harm of a specific error is higher than the aggregate measure shows.

### Phase 5: Redesign and validate the offer

**Owners:** Pricing and Packaging, Product Marketing, Finance, Customer Insights, Trust
**Governing rule:** One customer job per offer. Software and human help do not appear as five equal choices.

The current $49, $119, $199, and $699 prices are frozen as old hypotheses. Current competitors place broad self-serve tools from roughly the low $20s to $40 monthly in the reviewed set, while continuing Radar has a direct competitor at $19.90 monthly. Human services can support much higher prices only when credentials, scope, deliverables, and proof are concrete.

**Recommended architecture to test, not approve yet:**

| Offer hypothesis | Price hypothesis | One primary job | Commercial form |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Try It Free | $0, no card | Check one real job and keep the explanation | Acquisition and proof |
| Keep Watch | $24 monthly or $59 for three months | Alert me only when something meaningfully better appears | Recurring software |
| Active Search | $99 for 30 days, no automatic renewal | Help me choose and prepare for the right jobs during an active search | Time-bounded software |
| Guided Help | Test $499 for a tightly bounded beta; retain $699 only if fuller delivery is required | Work with me on one tightly defined decision and pursuit | Human service, qualification required |
| Expert Review | Test $199 only when the product exposes a meaningful unresolved decision | Have a qualified person review this specific decision | Contextual add-on, not a public peer tier |

**Pricing-page question:** What do you need right now?

**Validation sequence:**

1. Require at least 80 percent comprehension of what is free, what renews, what includes a person, and what happens next.
2. Deliver one real free decision before showing an upgrade.
3. Test $19, $24, and $29 monthly in separate cohorts while keeping packaging and copy stable.
4. Test $59 for three months only after monthly value is understood. State the total and savings plainly.
5. Offer a disclosed guided beta only after Board approval. Test $499 with a tightly bounded scope and retain $699 as the fuller-service alternative; then decide whether to retain, raise, narrow, or stop from observed delivery cost and demand.
6. Test Expert Review contextually only after the software shows why a human check might change the decision.
7. Measure four-week Radar opens, irrelevant recommendations, useful no-action results, actions, cancellations, support time, and direct cost.

**Economic stop rules:** Rework the offer if recurring variable cost exceeds 20 percent of collected plan revenue, guided delivery cannot achieve at least 40 percent direct contribution, or users cannot explain renewal and scope without assistance.

The offer architecture applies the local Sultanic and Hormozi guidance: fix product results before promotion, state a vivid outcome, organize components as a path rather than a pile, give each rung one job, separate continuity from expert ascension, and change one pricing variable at a time.

### Phase 6: Moderated usability and release gate

**Owners:** Customer Insights, Research Operations, Accessibility, Independent Quality
**Cohorts:**

- established active seekers,
- established passive professionals,
- career changers or level-up professionals,
- broader-language check with early-career or hourly workers.

Use at least five participants for each important cohort before claiming that cohort is well served. External recruitment requires Board approval.

**Core tasks:**

1. Explain the product after Home.
2. Paste a valid and invalid link and a job description.
3. Complete onboarding and revise an earlier answer.
4. Interpret recommendation, confidence, reason, risk, and unknowns.
5. Find pay, equity, benefits, requirements, and missing information.
6. Correct an AI mistake and understand what changed.
7. Choose Pursue, Watch, or Pass.
8. Select an offer and explain charge, term, renewal, cancellation, and human involvement.
9. Configure and later change Radar preferences.
10. Review materials and the exact external-action approval.

**Release gate:**

- zero open P0 or P1 defects,
- zero known P2 defects that materially impair a core task,
- at least 80 percent homepage comprehension,
- at least 85 percent unassisted onboarding completion,
- at least 85 percent decision comprehension,
- at least 80 percent offer and billing comprehension,
- core-task Single Ease Question average of at least 6 out of 7,
- successful keyboard, VoiceOver or TalkBack, screen-reader semantics, contrast, reduced-motion, and 200 percent zoom paths,
- no horizontal overflow, clipped action, trapped scroll, or keyboard-covered primary action,
- all approved adverse job fixtures pass,
- every external action remains separate, exact, and approval-gated.

## Homepage information architecture

The public website should use this order:

1. Plain outcome and two entry actions.
2. The problem in the visitor's language.
3. A four-step explanation of how My Way Ahead helps.
4. One realistic job-decision example.
5. Respectful comparison with job boards, trackers, resume scanners, and volume-application tools.
6. Simple trust behavior: fresh sources, explicit unknowns, private information, correction, and exact approval.
7. Paths for active search, passive monitoring, level-up, and career change.
8. Clear free value and a preview of paid support.
9. Founder case-study method and later verified outcomes.
10. Objections and FAQ.
11. Final action.

Provisional hero direction:

> **Find better jobs. Know which ones are worth pursuing.**
>
> Paste a job or tell us what you want next. My Way Ahead compares the pay, requirements, work style, and career growth, explains why the job may fit, and helps you take the next step.

## Human capacity sequence

No hire or contractor is approved by this plan. If evidence later justifies human capacity, the recommended order is:

1. Use virtual roles to repair and evaluate the synthetic prototype at zero cash cost.
2. Buy only bounded independent expertise at a real gate: senior mobile accessibility review, privacy or employment counsel, and security review.
3. First core product hire after alpha evidence: a founding full-stack product engineer capable of secure production delivery.
4. First product-craft hire: a senior product designer and researcher when repeated testing and design-system work exceed virtual capacity.
5. Add customer success and career operations when support approaches the 15-minute monthly guardrail or guided delivery becomes recurring.
6. Add dedicated AI or data engineering when source ingestion, ontology, and evaluation maintenance are no longer safely owned by the founding engineer.
7. Add dedicated growth only after activation and repeated use are credible.
8. Add fractional finance and operations when billing, vendors, refunds, and reporting become real.
