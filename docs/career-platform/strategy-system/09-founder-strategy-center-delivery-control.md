# 09 Founder Strategy Center and Delivery Control

## Control decision

The strategy system needs an operating surface, not a folder of documents. The Founder Strategy Center is the current decision cockpit for Matt and the product leader. It does not replace source documents; it shows what matters now, why, who owns it, and what decision is next.

The first version may be a generated internal report. Before the owned functional alpha it becomes a durable operator surface backed by the same versioned records used for cases and experiments.

### CEO autonomy and Board gates

The CEO may autonomously conduct research, organize experts, create or revise reversible local artifacts, prototype with synthetic data, run tests, perform QA, and recommend decisions within the approved goal and cost boundary.

Matt's explicit Board approval is required before selecting or publicly locking the brand; buying a domain; filing a trademark; incurring legal expense; selecting or combining the visual direction that authorizes implementation; spending money; enabling paid APIs, sources, vendors, or acquisition; public publishing; processing production customer data; activating billing; committing prices or refund terms; recruiting external testers; making public claims; hiring; partnering; sharing data; entering contracts; submitting job applications; changing Teal externally; sending outreach; using references; or negotiating.

Pause or roll back immediately after any critical fabrication, false-active job, privacy or security incident, misleading billing state, unauthorized external action, material brand collision, source-rights problem, or failed trust gate. Do not use a competitor white-label as the core product. Keep the product narrow and human-supervised if hidden operator work is required for self-serve success. Do not use unsupported outcome or fixed-time claims, or real sensitive data in an insecure prototype.

## Decision and issue card

Every material card uses the same contract:

- plain-language change or issue,
- user and business impact,
- source and observation window,
- verified fact, inference, and unknown separated,
- confidence and evidence limitation,
- affected users, cases, objects, and versions,
- recommendation and alternatives,
- owner and authority required,
- exact next action,
- acceptance or containment evidence,
- follow-up result and supersession link.

This prevents a confident narrative from replacing the underlying evidence.

## Reusable initiative brief

Before design or implementation, a brief must state:

1. user job and desired outcome,
2. current behavior and evidence,
3. belief or behavior shift,
4. journey entry, exit, pause, and return,
5. information hierarchy and source of truth,
6. components and interaction semantics,
7. empty, loading, partial, stale, conflict, error, success, and no-action states,
8. mobile, desktop, keyboard, accessibility, and theme behavior,
9. data, privacy, approval, and deletion boundaries,
10. event and metric contract,
11. acceptance evidence,
12. cost, stop condition, and rollback,
13. explicit out-of-scope items.

## Work-in-progress control

Way Ahead uses adaptive, evidence-based work in progress rather than a permanent numeric initiative cap. Activate a workstream only when it has one measurable result, one DRI, one writer per overlapping surface, bounded dependencies, tenant data, and write scope, acceptance evidence, available review capacity, cost and rollback boundaries, and a stop condition. One integrator owns sequencing and synthesis. Queue or pause work when those conditions fail; task count is observed portfolio state, not policy.

- One selected visual direction at a time.
- One writer for overlapping strategy or prototype files.
- One active implementation slice per shared surface.
- Matt's job-search work and company validation may run in parallel, but neither may mutate the other's state.
- External applications, outreach, payments, publishing, data sharing, and source commitments retain their separate approval gates.
- A newly discovered direct competitor, trust incident, or source constraint can return an initiative to `briefed` or `proposed`; sunk effort does not overrule evidence.

## Trust incident model

| Severity | Example | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Stop event | Unsupported external claim, unapproved action, privacy or tenant incident, critical false-active job | Pause affected acquisition and external action, preserve versions, contain, notify the owner, verify rollback before resuming |
| Major defect | Material score or source error that could change a decision but has not left the product | Mark dependents stale, adjudicate, correct, retest the affected slice |
| Local defect | Understandability, theme, responsive, or noncritical extraction issue | Log, prioritize by user impact, fix and verify without broad rollback |
| Improvement | Helpful but nonblocking enhancement | Keep outside WIP until a current gate or metric justifies it |

An incident record includes discovery source, affected versions, user impact, containment, correction, notification decision, root cause, regression test, owner, and closure evidence. No incident closes because the interface merely looks corrected.

## Source, model, and cost health

The operator surface separates:

- source availability, canonical identity, freshness, duplicate, and capture completeness,
- extraction quality by critical field and slice,
- abstention and human-review change,
- provider capacity, latency, retry, terms, privacy class, and cost,
- operator minutes and exception burden,
- queued work and degraded state.

Capacity failure never changes truth policy. Work is queued, rerouted to a task-qualified safe provider, handled manually, or returned as not processed. It is never converted into a lower-confidence answer that appears complete.

## Founder approval register

Matt retains approval for:

- brand selection, domain purchase, trademark or legal spend,
- public positioning and outcome claims,
- pricing commitments and paid offers,
- external publishing beyond the one approved private prototype checkpoint,
- vendor, source, or paid API commitments,
- hiring, partnership, or data-sharing decisions,
- job applications, outreach, references, and external negotiation.

The Strategy Center should ask for an approval only when the exact action, destination, cost, consequence, and rollback are visible.
