# 05 Business Model, Validation, and Growth

> **Current offer authority, 2026-07-21:** The market reasoning and validation design in this chapter remain useful. All older `$49/$119/$199/$699` and `$24/$59/$199/$499` tables are preserved as decision history. The current private hypotheses are governed by [16 Founder Feedback Product Reset](16-founder-feedback-product-reset-2026-07-21.md) and the [Unit Economics Ledger](../company-os/my-way-ahead-unit-economics-ledger-2026-07-21.md): $9/$24 Watch, $19/$49 Multi-Path Watch, $99/$149 30-day Active, and $39/$49 contextual software packs. Human services are paused. No price, billing, or public margin claim is approved.

## Business decision

Launch an owned, mobile-first decision-and-pursuit product with a narrow self-serve core and optional expert help. Build the smallest trustworthy software that completes the first valuable job end to end; do not postpone the company into a service-only fork, and do not assume that Opportunity Radar earns recurring billing without renewal evidence.

The first sellable outcome is:

> **Bring one real opportunity. Leave knowing whether it deserves pursuit, why, what could break the fit, and the strongest honest path forward.**

The product is designed for recurring value through active-search decisions, monitoring, career memory, readiness, and outcome learning. A monthly and three-month subscription should be tested early because the category and the search cycle support it. Retention must still be earned through full-price renewal and confirmed useful outcomes, not assumed from the existence of recurring features.

RoleWorth already sells a low-cost decision-first job-search product, while Waypoint sells human-built senior career intelligence from a one-time entry offer. Worklore, Umon, and CareerLedger also occupy persistent career memory, evidence, and experienced-professional positioning. The beta must therefore prove both a meaningful paid job and superior execution on the workflows the target user expects.

## Beachhead

Start narrower than `experienced professionals`:

- U.S. growth, revenue, GTM operations, lifecycle, ecommerce, and adjacent marketing leaders,
- roughly 8 to 20 years of experience,
- earning or targeting approximately $120,000 to $250,000,
- employed, recently displaced, or actively considering a specific role,
- one live opportunity or a shortlist of two to four,
- material ambiguity about mandate, proof, compensation, readiness, or career direction,
- rejects mass apply and values time, credibility, economics, and control.

This beachhead fits Matt's knowledge, sources, network, and first case. It reduces taxonomy breadth and makes expert judgment credible. Expansion requires evidence that the method transfers to another function or income segment.

Do not lead the business with passive professionals. Their need is plausible but less urgent, observable wins arrive more slowly, and free alternatives are abundant.

| Premium beta | Guided Career Move | First five at $699 for the defined beta term | Product access, proof interview, up to three human-reviewed opportunity decisions, one approval-ready pursuit, and structured feedback; exact scope and no automatic renewal |

The 20-decision and two-pursuit allowances are cost-control hypotheses, not artificial engagement targets. Deterministic checks, saving, and user-owned data should not be metered. Expensive named outputs may be bounded visibly; never sell abstract tokens or surprise overages.

During validation, the founding Guided beta ends without automatic renewal. The product's three-month subscription may renew only when the checkout shows the exact charge, term, next charge date, reminder policy, cancellation path, and post-cancellation data access. No annual or lifetime plan launches until a mature cohort proves value beyond an active search.

## Why three months deserves a first-class test

The category is naturally episodic, but a serious search and transition often lasts longer than one month. Teal, Huntr, Simplify, Careerflow, and Jobscan all validate customer familiarity with a three-month option. A three-month term can improve cash collection, give the product enough time to demonstrate compounding memory, and reduce monthly cancellation decisions.

The counter-risk is prepaid regret. The product must not manufacture activity to defend engagement. Measure plan selection, activation, refunds, support burden, no-action usefulness, term completion, renewal, perceived time saved, and decision quality. If three-month buyers activate less, complain more, or stop using the product after the first decision, the term is not working even if cash collection improves.

## Value equation and offer design

The offer increases value by improving:

- **Dream outcome:** a materially better career move, not another application.
- **Perceived likelihood:** clear evidence, canonical source, expert method, corrections, and no invented claims.
- **Time to value:** first useful decision before exhaustive profile completion.
- **Effort and sacrifice:** progressive onboarding, fewer roles, less duplicate work, and operator help during beta.

The main customer obstacles become product or service components:

| Obstacle | Solution component |
|---|---|
| I do not know whether the role is real or still open | Opportunity Integrity Preview |
| I cannot tell whether it is actually better | Career Baseline and Move Value |
| The job description is ambiguous | Atomic interpretation and uncertainty |
| I do not know what I can credibly claim | Evidence review and proof interview |
| I am unsure whether a gap is fatal or transferable | Pursuit Readiness and bounded gap plan |
| My tools are fragmented | Decision Receipt and Pursuit workspace |
| I distrust AI output | Sources, corrections, approval, and expert escalation |
| I do not want another endless subscription | Defined term, pause, credits, and transparent renewal |

Risk reversal should be narrow and observable. Examples to test:

- If the service cannot produce a decision-complete review because the source or evidence is insufficient, credit the payment toward another qualified role or refund the scoped review.
- Do not guarantee interviews, offers, salary increases, placement time, or hiring probability.

## Conservative unit economics

These are red-team planning assumptions, not observed economics. Labor is an economic cost even when Matt performs it.

| Offer scenario | Planning assumption | Contribution implication |
|---|---|---|
| Free first Decision Review | Target deterministic, model, source, storage, and sampled-QA cost below $2 per eligible account | Treat as product-led acquisition cost; do not include routine human delivery or allow repeated free identities to create unbounded cost |
| $49 Pro Monthly | Target model, source, storage, payment, and attributable support cost below $9.80 | At least 80% gross contribution before fixed overhead; support should stay below about 12 minutes per user per month |
| $119 Pro 3-Month | Monthly-equivalent revenue of $39.67; target variable cost below $7.93 per active month | At least 80% gross contribution before fixed overhead; unused prepaid obligations and refunds remain visible liabilities |
| $199 Expert Decision Review | One hour at $90 plus $15 tools, fees, and correction reserve | About $94 direct contribution, 47% before overhead; scope drift quickly destroys margin |
| $699 Guided beta | Five hours at $90 plus $60 tools, fees, and support reserve | About $189 contribution, 27% before overhead; acceptable only as a clearly labeled learning subsidy |
| Post-beta-only $1,249 Guided scenario | Five hours at $90 plus up to $112 inference, source, payment, and support cost | About $687 contribution, 55% before overhead; not part of the current prototype, checkout, founding offer, or approved public price |

The largest early cost is unlikely to be ordinary model inference. It is human review, source acquisition and verification, support, corrections, exceptions, and trust recovery. Track provider calls and tokens, but optimize the whole delivery path.

Cost control must preserve one quality floor. Free users receive less quantity, not permission for lower-integrity answers. Run deterministic extraction and validation first, cache public-job normalization, route only uncertain or consequential fields to a qualified model, cap spend by user and organization, and fail into `needs evidence` rather than spending without limit or fabricating certainty. Confidential career data never enters a free provider tier whose terms are unsafe for that data class.

At high churn, a low-priced Monitor cannot support expensive acquisition. Warm, referral, community, and partner distribution are the correct starting channels.

## Acquisition strategy

### Priority order

1. Matt's warm network in the initial functions.
2. Former colleagues, fractional leaders, and senior operators facing one live opportunity.
3. Customer referrals requested only after a useful decision.
4. Proof-led public teardowns that show why an attractive role was stale, risky, or not worth the effort.
5. Partnerships with independent career coaches and outplacement practitioners.
6. Two focused communities where senior growth, revenue, lifecycle, and operations leaders already discuss transitions.

### Best first lead magnet

> **Bring one role you are considering. See whether it is current, canonical, duplicated, and complete enough for a real decision before you invest further.**

This begins as the free Integrity Preview and, after account creation plus the minimum Baseline, leads to one complete self-serve decision. Human adjudication remains separately disclosed and paid. The bounded first decision is more credible than another personality assessment, generic resume scan, or broad salary report and gives the user enough product evidence to judge the subscription.

### Avoid initially

- paid acquisition before at least 20 collected purchases,
- broad AI career-coach content,
- generic resume SEO,
- recruiter-funded placement before independence is understood,
- mass free concierge work,
- outcome-based claims without evidence,
- multi-sided marketplace development.

## Owned-product and infrastructure decision

The company will build and own its software product. It does not need to reinvent every technical dependency. Use the prototype and early customers to establish the method and a portable, versioned data contract, then make explicit buy-versus-build decisions for commodity layers.

| Layer choice | When it wins | Main risk and control |
|---|---|---|
| Build the owned proprietary core | Customer-facing strategy, baseline, evidence, opportunity interpretation, decision policy, corrections, Pursuit, and learning define the product | Scope creep; control with thin vertical slices, benchmarks, and explicit WIP |
| Integrate commodity infrastructure | A provider supplies hosting, authentication, billing, email, permitted source access, or qualified model capacity faster and more safely | Dependency and data risk; control with portable schemas, vendor qualification, export, fallback, and switching plans |
| Add expert delivery | Human judgment improves consequential decisions, creates early revenue, and exposes repeatable work | Service work can mask weak UX; track every intervention and keep it optional and separately priced |

Licensing or white-labeling another career platform as the core company is out. No new vendor spend, data sharing, payment, or external contact is authorized by this strategy alone. The purpose of integrations is to avoid wasting limited resources on commodity infrastructure while preserving ownership of the product and customer relationship.

## Activation

Profile completion is not activation. A match score is not activation.

A user is activated when:

1. one real job or real target direction is captured,
2. the minimum Career Baseline exists,
3. a small number of high-stakes facts are confirmed,
4. the user receives a reviewable recommendation with uncertainty,
5. the user accepts or meaningfully corrects it,
6. the decision changes, confirms, or avoids a real action.

Primary activation metric:

> **Qualified users who reach a trusted first decision and confirm that it changed, validated, or prevented a real next action.**

## Retention hypotheses

### Active-search retention

- weekly portfolio triage,
- progress on evidence gaps,
- fewer repeated inputs,
- better next-action clarity,
- outcome and objection learning,
- grounded preparation across several pursuits.

### Passive retention

- material-change alerts rather than daily volume,
- monthly market-position and compensation movement,
- achievement and proof capture,
- periodic baseline refresh,
- included deep-decision credits,
- easy pause and reactivation.

Opportunity Radar is one recurring-value capability inside the product, not the entire subscription. Recurring billing remains an experiment until full-price monthly renewal, three-month term behavior, repeat decisions, and no-action value are observed.

## Customer discovery and paid-beta method

### Recent-behavior interview

Ask about one real recent decision, not abstract preferences:

- What triggered the search or opportunity review?
- Which tools and people did you use?
- Where did the process become uncertain or repetitive?
- What did you decide, and what evidence changed the decision?
- What was the economic or emotional cost of getting it wrong?
- What happened after you applied or passed?
- What would you pay to solve that specific problem again?

Show a generic match card and the full Decision Receipt in randomized order. Measure which one changes a decision and which components create value.

### Paid-beta invitation

Describe the exact deliverable, scope, evidence boundary, price, and what is not guaranteed. Ask for collected payment or a deposit. Praise, waitlist sign-up, and stated willingness do not count as paid demand.

## Continue and stop gates

These are planning hypotheses for small early cohorts, not statistically validated thresholds. Report exact counts, observation windows, material exceptions, and uncertainty. Do not convert a passing small cohort into an efficacy or market-size claim.

### Problem gate

Continue if, among ten qualified interviews:

- at least seven describe a recent costly opportunity decision,
- at least five already use multiple tools or paid help,
- at least five say the Decision Receipt solves something their stack does not.

### Activation gate

Continue if, among five alpha users:

- at least four accept or materially correct the decision,
- at least three change, confirm, or avoid a real action,
- no critical fact is fabricated,
- every unknown remains visibly unknown.

### Paid-demand gate

Continue the product subscription if:

- at least five of 25 qualified activated users pay the fully disclosed monthly or three-month price,
- payment is collected,
- at least three subscribers reach a second trusted decision or a Pursuit action,
- no more than one of the first ten purchasers is refunded for product or billing failure,
- purchase does not depend on hidden discounts or misrepresented alternatives.

Continue the expert add-on if at least three of 12 qualified prospects pay for the $199 Expert Review or $699 Guided beta with the exact scope disclosed. Track software demand and expert-service demand separately.

### Delivery-economics gate

Continue the owned software slice if, by ten paying users:

- subscription variable cost remains no more than 20% of collected subscription revenue,
- median attributable support remains below 15 minutes per paying user per month,
- material operator rescue declines rather than becoming the hidden product,
- repeated failures are concentrated enough to fix or automate.

For expert delivery, continue only when the tested scope achieves at least 40% contribution at a sustainable price. Otherwise narrow scope, raise price, or keep the add-on capacity limited.

### Subscription gate

Keep the three-month plan as a primary choice only if, among at least ten checkout-eligible prospects and then a matured purchaser cohort:

- at least 30% of checkout-eligible purchasers choose it without a deeper hidden discount,
- three-month buyers activate at least as well as monthly buyers,
- refunds and billing complaints do not materially exceed the monthly cohort,
- matured buyers use the product beyond the first decision and understand renewal before it occurs.

Continue recurring Monitoring only when monthly renewal, three-month term completion, no-action usefulness, and irrelevant-role rate show that value survives quiet periods. If active-search use ends but Monitoring does not retain, keep the subscription search-focused and use pause, reactivation, or episodic decisions instead of manufacturing alerts.

### Trust and safety gate

Pause acquisition after:

- any exported unsupported claim,
- any unapproved external action,
- any critical active classification for a closed or invalid role,
- any privacy, security, or tenant-isolation incident.

### 90-day business gate

Continue investing if:

- at least ten people have paid across software and expert offers, with each revenue type reported separately,
- at least $5,000 total has been collected and at least $1,000 comes from software subscriptions rather than expert labor,
- software subscribers show repeat use beyond the free decision,
- subscription variable cost is no more than 20% of subscription revenue and expert cases achieve at least 40% contribution at their standard tested price,
- three repeatable referral sources exist,
- five non-founder cases show the mechanism works outside Matt.

At that gate, decide which owned-product slice to deepen next, which commodity layers to integrate, and how much expert support to retain. The decision to build the company's own product is already settled.

## Metrics system

### North Star

> **Trusted career decisions that change, confirm, or prevent a consequential user action.**

### Metric hierarchy

| Type | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | Qualified conversations, source, integrity-preview starts, free-decision starts, monthly and three-month conversion, expert-add-on conversion, collected revenue |
| Activation | First decision reached, accepted or corrected, action changed or confirmed, time and operator effort to first value |
| Decision quality | Source coverage, candidate coverage, corrections, abstentions, decision-changing unknowns, irrelevant surfaced rate |
| Delivery | Operator minutes by stage, model and source cost, review loops, support minutes, defect and incident rate |
| Funnel | Integrity Preview to account, account to free first decision, first decision to subscription, plan mix, decision to Expert Review or Guided beta, Pursuit, and stage outcomes |
| Retention | Repeat decisions, month-two renewal, three-month term completion and renewal, paid reactivation, career-memory reuse, no-action value, pause and return |
| Economics | Collected revenue, gross contribution by offer, refund, payment cost, acquisition cost, support burden |
| Outcomes | Screens, interviews, stage progress, offers, accepted compensation, time saved, prevented bad pursuits |
| Guardrails | Unsupported claims, source false-active, approval violations, privacy incidents, protected-trait leakage, billing complaints |

Do not treat interviews, offers, or salary changes as caused by the product without a valid design. Report observed association and method limits.

## Learning record

Every material experiment records:

- question,
- target persona and journey stage,
- prior belief,
- source and sample,
- version and observation window,
- result,
- confidence and limitation,
- decision,
- owner,
- next test or revisit trigger.

This prevents smooth narratives from overwriting inconvenient evidence.

## Biggest business failure modes

1. Recurring billing conflicts with episodic need.
2. Free tools copy visible features and weaken the message.
3. Proof setup costs more effort than users tolerate.
4. One incorrect consequential recommendation destroys trust.
5. Reliable source freshness and duplicate control remain brittle.
6. Human judgment remains necessary and margins never improve.
7. The target remains too broad across professions.
8. Matt's expertise does not transfer outside the first lanes.
9. No low-cost repeatable acquisition channel emerges.
10. Users learn the method, then return to free tools.
11. Sensitive career data creates security and reputational exposure.
12. Founder success is mistaken for market demand.
13. Outcome learning is slow and confounded.
14. Building the full suite consumes the resources needed to sell one valuable decision.
15. Always-on engagement increases anxiety or notification fatigue.
16. Employer or recruiter revenue compromises user alignment.
