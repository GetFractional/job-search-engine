# Brand Name Shortlist

Date: 2026-07-17

Status: one candidate advances to validation; no name is legally cleared

## Leader Decision

**Worthward remains the strongest candidate despite the spelling concern.** The concern is real, not cosmetic: the `th-w` transition can sound like `Worthword`, and `ward` has hospital, guardian, political-district, and direction-suffix associations. It still wins because it expresses movement toward something worth pursuing, fits the product promise, and had the cleanest preliminary collision profile.

Do not force three names into equal status. The decision-ready ranking is:

1. **Worthward:** advance to customer comprehension, audio spelling, and formal clearance.
2. **ConsiderNext:** retain as the functional backup.
3. **Nextworthy:** retain only as an emotional-safety test and backup.

If Worthward fails the spoken-name or formal-clearance gate, run another creative round before settling for either backup.

## Why Worthward Still Leads

### Hormozi association lens

The name can accumulate the right associations if the product repeatedly pairs it with better decisions, avoided wasted effort, stronger career economics, and user control. It should never be paired with hype, mass applying, or personal-worth judgment.

### Sultanic mechanism lens

The coined name cannot carry the whole explanation. At launch, always pair it with a concrete mechanism or promise:

> Worthward compares real opportunities with where you are now, verifies what you can prove, and helps you act only when the move holds up.

### Product-system fit

The name works above:

- Worthward Radar,
- Worthward Decision Receipt,
- Worthward Career Baseline,
- Worthward Pursuit Sprint.

`Worthward Radar` is understandable only after the product category is explained, so early acquisition should use `Worthward, the always-on career decision platform` rather than the name alone.

## Worthward Validation Protocol

Use 12 to 20 target users across the Ambitious Pathfinder and Active Optimizer personas. Do not show the logo before the audio test.

### Test 1: hear-once spelling

Read this sentence once:

> I use Worthward to decide which career opportunities are worth pursuing.

Ask the participant to type the product name without help.

Advance gate:

- at least 80 percent type `Worthward` correctly,
- no more than 10 percent type a `word` variant,
- no repeated explanation is required.

If the name fails narrowly, test whether visual exposure reliably fixes recall before rejecting it. If it fails badly, do not try to solve the problem with advertising spend.

### Test 2: five-second category expectation

Show only the wordmark and descriptor for five seconds. Ask:

- What do you think this product does?
- Who is it for?
- What result would you expect?
- What would make you distrust it?

Advance only if most respondents describe career opportunity evaluation or better career moves, not writing, finance, hospitals, recruiting software, or self-esteem coaching.

### Test 3: emotional safety

Ask recently laid-off, underemployed, and currently employed participants:

- Does the name judge your worth, or the opportunity's worth?
- Does it feel supportive, clinical, financial, or pressuring?
- Would you trust it with compensation and career-history data?

The opportunity must be the object being judged. Any recurring personal-worth interpretation is a stop signal.

### Test 4: real product sentence

Randomize these messages:

- `Worthward found one opportunity worth reviewing.`
- `Nothing currently clears your Worthward career standard.`
- `Your Worthward Decision Receipt is ready.`
- `Worthward found no move that improves on where you are now.`

Measure comprehension, premium trust, interest in opening, and whether the no-action messages still feel valuable.

### Test 5: delayed recall

Ask for the name and spelling again at the end of the interview or the next day. Immediate recognition alone is not enough.

## Formal Clearance Ladder

Only after the user test passes:

1. comprehensive USPTO word-mark and phonetic search across relevant classes,
2. state and federal company-name search,
3. common-law search for unregistered use,
4. domain and confusing-variant review,
5. app-store and social-handle review,
6. foreign-register search for planned launch markets,
7. trademark counsel's likelihood-of-confusion opinion,
8. only then acquire, file, announce, or spend materially.

## Decision Rule

- **Advance Worthward** only if it passes spoken spelling, category comprehension, emotional safety, and formal clearance.
- **Use ConsiderNext** only if the team intentionally accepts weaker distinctiveness for clearer function.
- **Use Nextworthy** only if interviews show that users consistently judge the opportunity, not themselves.
- **Run another round** if Worthward fails. Do not lower the standard because a shortlist exists.
