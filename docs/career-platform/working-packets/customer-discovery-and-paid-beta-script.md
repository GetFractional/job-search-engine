# Phase 2 Customer Discovery And Paid Beta Script

Status: executable staging script; no invitations sent
Date: 2026-07-17
Working product name: Job Filter

## Purpose

Recruit five qualified alpha users and ten paid-beta prospects, learn whether the Decision Receipt solves a real purchasing problem, and avoid mistaking compliments for demand.

## Participant Criteria

Prioritize people who meet at least four of these conditions:

- currently earn or target $90,000 to $250,000,
- have at least seven years of professional experience,
- are employed, recently displaced, or actively considering a specific role,
- have more than one plausible career lane or a meaningful level-up decision,
- care about compensation, benefits, logistics, and long-term optionality,
- have tried alerts, LinkedIn, Teal, ChatGPT, a resume tool, coaching, or spreadsheets,
- can bring one current job URL or full job description,
- will correct facts and review evidence rather than asking for mass application.

Exclude people whose immediate goal is maximum application volume, hourly or shift-work discovery, employer recruiting, legal or immigration adjudication, or resume design alone.

## Research Rules

- Ask about a recent real decision, not hypothetical preferences.
- Ask what happened before asking what someone wants.
- Do not explain the product until the participant's existing behavior and costs are understood.
- Do not sell a subscription before recurring pain is established.
- Do not offer a discount in exchange for praise.
- Record exact customer language separately from interpretation.
- Ask permission before recording, storing documents, or using an anonymized case.
- Never promise interviews, offers, compensation, or time to hire.

## Thirty-Minute Interview

### 1. Context, 3 minutes

1. What is happening in your career that made this conversation relevant now?
2. Are you actively searching, quietly monitoring, or deciding between directions?
3. What would make a new role materially better than your current situation?

Capture:

- search state,
- current and target lane,
- compensation and family-benefit threshold,
- logistics and hard constraints,
- urgency trigger.

### 2. Recent Behavior, 8 minutes

1. Tell me about the last job you seriously considered.
2. Where did you find it?
3. How did you decide whether it was worth pursuing?
4. What did you check before investing time?
5. Which tools, people, or files did you use?
6. How long did the decision and preparation take?
7. What information was missing or hard to trust?
8. What happened afterward?

Follow-ups:

- Have you ever prepared for a role that turned out to be filled, stale, duplicated, underpaid, or different from the visible employer?
- Have alerts ever produced a role that was genuinely worth acting on?
- What do you repeatedly redo for every role?
- What is the most expensive mistake you have made in a search, in time, money, confidence, or lost opportunity?

### 3. Alternatives And Dissatisfaction, 5 minutes

1. What have you already tried to solve this?
2. What did each option do well?
3. Where did it break down?
4. What do you still do manually after using it?
5. What would make you stop trusting a recommendation?
6. What would make you return every week after getting a job?

Do not criticize Teal, LinkedIn, ChatGPT, coaches, or other products. Learn the participant's real division of labor among them.

After unaided recall, ask whether the participant has used or seriously considered a free or low-cost passive-monitoring alternative such as WhyBrilliant, Sintovia, LinkedIn alerts, or another AI job radar. Record what, if anything, they would still pay an independent decision product to do.

### 4. Live Decision Test, 8 minutes

Ask the participant to provide one current opportunity. Walk through a concierge Decision Receipt containing:

- canonical employer and source,
- live, stale, duplicate, wrapper, or unknown state,
- compensation, benefits, location, and travel,
- improvement over the current-career baseline,
- requirements proven, plausible, missing, risky, and disqualifying,
- proof used and excluded,
- expected effort and main uncertainty,
- read-only system recommendation plus a `Pursue`, `Watch`, or `Pass` user decision,
- one next action.

Then ask:

1. What surprised you?
2. What appears wrong or incomplete?
3. Which part changed or confirmed your decision?
4. What evidence would you need before trusting this?
5. What would you have done without this review?
6. How much time or effort did this save, if any?

Activation is not satisfaction. Activation occurs only when the participant accepts or meaningfully corrects the decision.

### 5. Purchase Test, 4 minutes

Only after the live decision has created value, show the relevant offer.

For active search:

> I am testing a guided 30-day sprint that includes three Decision Receipts, one approval-ready pursuit path, and four selective opportunity briefs. The first five places are $399 because I am still doing the work personally and measuring every step. Would you buy that for your current search?

For passive monitoring:

> I am testing a weekly service that sends zero to three opportunities only when they appear materially better or strategically useful. It is $24 per month. Would you use and pay for that after this first review?

After the initial answer, disclose the competitive anchors: WhyBrilliant currently markets a free candidate model funded by employer hiring fees, while Sintovia currently lists a daily Radar plan at $19.90 monthly. Ask what must be observably better or more independent for the proposed service to deserve payment. Do not hide free alternatives to manufacture paid intent.

If the answer is no, ask:

1. What makes it not worth buying?
2. Is the problem weak, the outcome weak, the timing wrong, the trust insufficient, or the price wrong?
3. What would you pay for instead, based on something you have bought before?

Do not count `maybe`, praise, waitlist signup, or a requested reminder as willingness to pay. Count a payment, deposit, or explicit scheduled purchase with a stated decision condition.

### 6. Close, 2 minutes

1. May we follow up about the decision and any downstream outcome?
2. May we use an anonymized version of the case, with a separate review before publication?
3. Who else has this exact problem and can bring a real opportunity?

No referral request is sent or acted on without Matt's approval of the exact recipient and message.

## Optional Post-Session Brand Test

Run this only after the product and purchase tests so the brand does not contaminate problem discovery.

1. Say `Worthward` once in a sentence and ask the participant to type the product name without seeing it.
2. Show `Worthward, the always-on career decision platform` for five seconds.
3. Ask what the product does, who it serves, and what it makes them feel.
4. Ask whether the name judges the opportunity or the person's worth.
5. Test the no-action sentence: `Worthward found no move that improves on where you are now.`
6. Ask for the name and spelling again at the end or in the follow-up.

Record exact spelling, `word` variants, category expectation, personal-worth interpretations, trust, and delayed recall. Do not ask only which name the participant likes.

## First Cohort Decision Gates

After five qualified alpha users:

- Continue if at least four reach a defensible accepted or corrected decision.
- Repair activation if fewer than four do.
- Do not build broader search, resume, tracker, or auto-apply features to compensate for a weak decision.

After offering the Guided Sprint to 15 qualified prospects:

- Continue the initial offer if at least five pay at least $399.
- Change the persona, offer, or value demonstration before adding features if fewer than five buy.
- Treat price as the problem only when participants clearly value the result and reject the economics.

After four weekly Radar briefs:

- Continue subscription testing if at least 60% review surfaced opportunities and fewer than 10% of surfaced roles are clearly irrelevant.
- Lead with episodic Sprint if fewer than 30% take a meaningful action in three of four weeks.
- A useful no-action brief may count as value only when the participant says it saved time or increased confidence.
