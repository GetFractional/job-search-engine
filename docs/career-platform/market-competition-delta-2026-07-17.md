# Market And Competition Delta

Research date: 2026-07-17

Status: official-claim screen, not efficacy validation

## Decision

The market has moved closer to the proposed product than the prior benchmark set suggested. `Always-on`, `career intelligence`, `job radar`, passive monitoring, evidence-backed career graphs, next-best actions, and low-noise interruption are now active competitor language.

The company should not try to own those features or phrases individually. The defensible wedge must be the inspectable decision contract that joins opportunity integrity, a user-approved Career Baseline, proof lineage, career economics, uncertainty, pursuit effort, and approval before external action.

## What Is Now Table Stakes

- persistent profile and preferences,
- continuous or daily job scanning,
- ranked matches,
- a short why-this-fits explanation,
- active and passive search modes,
- alert cadence controls,
- resume and cover-letter generation,
- one obvious next action,
- privacy and user-control language,
- a free or low-cost starting point.

`Opportunity Radar`, `Career Intelligence`, and `always-on` may remain useful descriptive language, but none should be presented as proprietary differentiation.

## Defensible Decision Contract

The product must make the following combination observable, not merely claim it:

1. **Career Baseline:** the current role, compensation, constraints, career capital, and acceptable move are user-approved.
2. **Opportunity integrity:** employer, requisition, source, duplicate, wrapper, availability, freshness, and ATS migration are explicit states.
3. **Proof lineage:** every externally usable claim points to a versioned, approved fact and source.
4. **Career economics:** compensation, benefits, risk, switching cost, career capital, and future optionality are compared with the baseline.
5. **Uncertainty and effort:** missing evidence, conflicting evidence, pursuit effort, and confidence are visible.
6. **Decision Receipt:** recommendation, rationale, invalidating risk, user decision, and one next action are portable and auditable.
7. **Approval boundary:** user decision and external-action approval remain separate.
8. **Calibration:** corrections and observed outcomes improve thresholds without inventing causal claims.

If a beta does not make at least the first six materially more useful than a free alert or generic match score, the subscription thesis has not been validated.

## Monetization Consequences

### Radar subscription

Do not charge merely for scanning and alerts. The recurring fee must buy:

- independent, source-verified decisions,
- a maintained Career Baseline and proof vault,
- economic and strategic comparison,
- low-noise thresholds the user can inspect and correct,
- continuity after a job change,
- a credible handoff into a Pursuit Sprint.

### Free boundary

The free entry should demonstrate one Decision Receipt or one Career Baseline diagnostic. Giving away undifferentiated alerts does not create the right conversion contrast when competitors already offer them free or cheaply.

### Paid beta

The concierge-assisted beta remains the fastest route to evidence because it can test whether users pay for judgment before engineering the monitoring infrastructure. The paid-beta interview must explicitly ask why the participant would pay instead of using WhyBrilliant, Sintovia, Teal, LinkedIn alerts, or ChatGPT.

## Go-To-Market Consequences

1. Keep the launch beachhead narrow: experienced U.S. professionals with multiple credible lanes, meaningful compensation upside, and costly proof or decision complexity.
2. Lead with an outcome contrast: fewer wasted pursuits and better-defended decisions, not more matches.
3. Demonstrate a real Decision Receipt against a real Career Baseline.
4. Publish proof of restraint, such as filled-role detection, suppressed duplicates, a no-action week, or a recommendation changed by missing evidence.
5. Avoid unsupported speed, interview, offer, salary, and causality claims.
6. Treat placement-funded competitors as an incentive-design contrast, not as inferior products. The independent subscription must prove that the user, not the employer, is the customer.

## Validation Tests Added

- Show the same person a generic ranked match and the full Decision Receipt. Ask which changes a decision and what part creates that difference.
- Present WhyBrilliant's free candidate model and Sintovia's current monthly price before asking willingness to pay.
- Test whether `independent`, `verified`, `compared with where you are now`, and `you approve every external action` increase paid intent.
- Test a no-opportunity week. Retention is credible only if users value confirmed restraint.
- Record which evidence or integrity state actually changes a pursue/pass decision.

## Evidence Boundary

All competitor descriptions above are their own current public claims. This screen does not confirm match quality, user counts, job coverage, placement outcomes, retention, revenue, or technical implementation. Prices and product claims may change and should be reverified before external publication or an investor-facing market claim.
